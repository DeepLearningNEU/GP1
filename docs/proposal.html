From Words to Worlds: Text-to-Image Generation with a Transformer Text Encoder and Latent Diffusion

IE 7615 Deep Learning for AI · Generative Project Proposal · Repository: github.com/DeepLearningNEU/GP1

1. Goal

We will build and study a text-to-image pipeline in which a Transformer text encoder conditions a diffusion model. Starting from a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model, we will fine-tune it on paired image–caption data, then measure how sampling and conditioning choices affect image quality, diversity, and text–image alignment.

2. Dataset

  • COCO 2017 Captions. Fine-tuning set: 5,000 randomly sampled train2017 image–caption pairs (seed 42). Evaluation set: all 5,000 val2017 images, used as the FID/IS reference distribution; their captions are the generation prompts.
  • Preprocessing. For each image we keep one caption, chosen at random among those with 5–30 words so that it fits CLIP's 77-token limit. Images are resized on the short side, center-cropped to 512×512, and normalized to [−1, 1]. Captions are tokenized and embedded with the CLIP text encoder.

3. Model and Method

  • Text encoder (Transformer): CLIP ViT-L/14 text encoder, a 12-layer Transformer with 123M parameters. It maps each caption to a 77×768 embedding sequence.
  • Generator (diffusion): Stable Diffusion v1.5. Its VAE compresses images to 64×64×4 latents, and a UNet denoiser reads the text embeddings through cross-attention.
  • Fine-tuning: LoRA adapters on the UNet attention layers, trained on the 5,000 COCO pairs with the standard noise-prediction (MSE) objective. The text encoder and VAE stay frozen.
  • Experiments: (i) noise schedulers (DDPM, DDIM, PNDM, DPM-Solver) × sampling steps (10–100); (ii) classifier-free guidance scale (1–15); (iii) conditioning strength; (iv) choice of text embedding; (v) base vs. LoRA-fine-tuned model.

4. Evaluation

Quantitative: FID and Inception Score on 5,000 images generated from the val2017 captions, plus CLIP score for text–image alignment. Guidance scale is swept to trace the fidelity–diversity trade-off. Qualitative: side-by-side grids of prompts and generations, and inspection of failure cases (counting, spatial relations, attribute binding). Ethics: discussion of dataset bias in COCO and in the pre-training data, and of misuse risks.

5. Tools, Compute, and Plan

Python, PyTorch, Hugging Face Diffusers / Transformers / PEFT, and torchmetrics. Jobs run on NEU Explorer HPC GPUs (V100 / P100) through SLURM.
