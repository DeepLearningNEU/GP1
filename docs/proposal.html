1. Dataset

  • Source: COCO 2017 Captions. Each image has five human-written captions.
    • Main set: 5,000 randomly sampled train2017 image–caption pairs (seed 42).
    • Evaluation set: all 5,000 val2017 images, used later as the FID/IS reference distribution.
  • Cleaning. For each image we keep one caption, chosen at random among those with 5–30 words. Very short or very long captions are discarded, so every kept caption fits the 77-token context of the CLIP text encoder. All 10,000 images downloaded and decoded without errors.
  • Image preprocessing. Images are resized on the short side and center-cropped to 512×512. For the diffusion model they are normalized to [−1, 1]. For the CLIP image encoder they are resized to 224×224 with CLIP's mean/std normalization.
  • Caption preprocessing. Captions are tokenized with the GPT-2 byte-level BPE tokenizer (vocabulary of 50,257). The end-of-text token is reused for padding, and captions are padded to a fixed length. They are also tokenized with the CLIP tokenizer for text conditioning.

2. Model

  • Image → embedding: CLIP ViT-L/14 vision Transformer. It splits each 224×224 image into 14×14-pixel patches (256 patches plus one [CLS] token), and the [CLS] output is projected to a 768-dimensional image embedding. Embeddings for all 5,000 images are pre-computed.
  • Text side: GPT-2 tokenizer for caption tokens. The CLIP ViT-L/14 text encoder (a 12-layer Transformer) maps a caption to a 77×768 embedding sequence in the same space as the image embeddings.
  • Generator: Stable Diffusion v1.5, a latent diffusion model. A VAE compresses images to 64×64×4 latents, and a UNet denoiser is conditioned on the text embeddings through cross-attention. We will fine-tune it on our COCO pairs with LoRA adapters on the UNet attention layers.
  • Planned experiments: noise schedulers and sampling steps, classifier-free guidance scale, conditioning strength, and choice of text embedding.
